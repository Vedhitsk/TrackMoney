The primary material

Your reference button is not a colour — it is a material, and it is built from four things. Three readings of it are below, differing only in how hard each layer is pushed. Your own image is loaded beside each one on the same near-black ground, so the comparison is direct rather than remembered.

1 · Gradient rampA short vertical shift, roughly one step of value, angled 176° so it is not perfectly plumb. Never so deep that the bottom edge reads black.
2 · Rim-lightA 1px inset highlight along the top edge and a 1px inset shadow along the bottom, so the control reads as lit from above rather than painted on.
3 · Coloured bloomThree stacked shadows in the brand hue, not grey: a tight contact shadow, a mid glow, and a wide diffuse halo. This is what makes it glow rather than float.
4 · Translucent tileThe glyph sits in a rounded square of white at low opacity with its own rim-light — a control within a control.

Two things I changed from my earlier attempt: the corner radius is a soft rounded-rect, not a full pill — your reference is roughly 0.28 of its own height, which is what gives it that squircle weight. And the bloom is far wider and softer than a normal shadow, which is the layer that was missing.
